Transformers deliver strong language modeling, but attention prefill grows quadratically with context and KV-cache memory grows linearly; fixed-state alternatives can sacrifice capability and still decode inefficiently on GPUs. We introduce Mamba-3, an inference-first state-space architecture that jointly revises recurrence, state dynamics, and input-output structure. Its exponential-trapezoidal recurrence adds learned trap interpolation, its complex-valued update realizes data-dependent rotations, and its rank-4 multi-input multi-output (MIMO) path increases arithmetic intensity during memory-bound decoding. We pretrain 180M–1.5B models on 100B FineWeb-Edu tokens with a shared Llama-3.1 tokenizer and 2K context. At 1.5B parameters, Mamba-3 MIMO obtains 10.24 perplexity and 57.6 average downstream accuracy, compared with 10.51/55.4 for a Transformer, 10.47/55.7 for Mamba-2, and 10.45/55.8 for Gated DeltaNet. The complex update also reaches 100.0%, 98.51%, and 87.75% accuracy on three formal state-tracking tasks, versus 0.9%, 47.81%, and 0.88% for Mamba-2. On H100 in BF16 at state size 128, the SISO kernel takes 0.156 ms per token, below Mamba-2's 0.203 ms, while MIMO takes 0.179 ms and offers the stronger quality point. Pure Mamba-3 remains weaker on some semi-structured and unstructured retrieval tasks, motivating hybrid attention when sparse-fact retention is essential. Together, these results show that state-space principles can improve both language-model quality and recurrent inference efficiency, with a quality-latency tradeoff controlled by SISO versus MIMO.
